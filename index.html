What this app watches for

  • Speed fade. If you finish much slower than you started, you were tensing up or started too fast.
  • Uneven rhythm. Big variation between taps means mashing. Clean, even taps are what transfer to real play.
  • Galloping. With two fingers, one finger landing late makes taps pair up (da-dum, da-dum). The app tells you which one.
  • Doubled taps. Hitting the same key twice while alternating means a finger is missing or you're flailing.
  • Pressing harder. If you hold keys down longer as the set goes on, your hand is tightening.
  • Form collapse during a set. With auto-stop on, the set ends as soon as speed or rhythm falls apart, so you don't rehearse bad reps.

A sensible session

  1. Calibrate on your first session and about once a week after. It sets your training zone.
  2. Warm up for 2–3 minutes at an easy metronome speed.
  3. One speed test to see where you are today. Don't max-test every set.
  4. Metronome sets inside your training zone (about 80–90% of max). Move up 5 BPM only when the app says the set was clean.
  5. Rest roughly twice as long as each set. The app counts it down for you.
  6. Stop for the day when quality drops two sets in a row. Tired reps teach tense habits.

Technique cues

  • Keep your wrist and forearm loose. Speed comes from relaxation, not force.
  • Press only as hard as the key needs. Long, heavy presses slow you down.
  • Short, frequent sessions beat one long grind.

If it hurts, stop

Burning, aching, numbness or tingling in your hand, wrist or forearm means stop for the day. Pain that lasts or comes back is worth seeing a doctor or physio about. Pushing through it is how repetitive strain injuries start. The app asks after every set, and if you report pain it tells you to rest for the day.
